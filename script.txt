# The History of AI - How Machines Became Intelligent

**[0:00 - 0:25 | HOOK]**

Imagine going back 80 years.

Computers were massive machines that could barely perform basic calculations.

And today?

You can talk to an AI, ask it to write code, create images, analyze videos, translate languages, and even solve complex problems.

So, how did we get here?

How did machines go from simply following instructions,

to something that looks like intelligence?

This is the story of Artificial Intelligence.

---

**[0:25 - 1:05 | THE IDEA BEFORE AI]**

The idea of artificial intelligence actually started long before ChatGPT, smartphones, or even modern computers.

In the 1940s and 1950s, scientists began asking a strange question:

"Can a machine think?"

One of the most important people in this story was Alan Turing.

In 1950, Turing published a famous paper called "Computing Machinery and Intelligence."

Instead of trying to define exactly what "thinking" means, he proposed a test.

If a human could communicate with a machine and couldn't reliably tell whether they were talking to a human or a machine,

maybe we could call that machine intelligent.

This became known as the Turing Test.

And this question became the foundation for a completely new field.

---

**[1:05 - 1:40 | THE BIRTH OF AI]**

Then came 1956.

A group of researchers met at Dartmouth College in the United States.

And they officially introduced the term:

"Artificial Intelligence."

The basic idea was incredibly ambitious:

What if intelligence could be described so precisely that a machine could simulate it?

Early AI researchers believed that with enough rules and computing power, machines could eventually solve many problems that humans solve using intelligence.

And initially, they weren't completely wrong.

Early programs could solve mathematical problems, play games, prove logical statements, and manipulate symbols.

But there was one huge problem.

These systems didn't really learn.

They were mostly following rules written by humans.

---

**[1:40 - 2:20 | RULES vs MACHINE LEARNING]**

Think about teaching a computer to recognize a cat.

The old approach would be something like:

"If it has two ears, four legs, whiskers, and a tail, then it's probably a cat."

But real life doesn't work like that.

Cats can look completely different.

So instead of giving the machine every rule, scientists started asking:

"What if the machine learns the rules itself?"

This idea became known as Machine Learning.

Instead of explicitly programming every decision, you give a system examples.

Thousands, millions, or eventually billions of examples.

The machine looks for patterns in the data and builds its own mathematical model.

And this changed everything.

---

**[2:20 - 3:00 | NEURAL NETWORKS]**

One of the most important ideas behind modern AI is the neural network.

Neural networks are loosely inspired by the way biological brains process information.

A neural network contains layers of mathematical operations.

You give it an input, the information passes through these layers, and eventually you get an output.

For example, give a neural network thousands of pictures of cats and dogs.

At first, its predictions are terrible.

But during training, the system compares its prediction with the correct answer.

Then it adjusts millions or even billions of internal parameters.

Again and again.

Prediction. Error. Adjustment. Repeat.

Eventually, the network becomes extremely good at recognizing patterns.

And this approach eventually led to something called Deep Learning.

---

**[3:00 - 3:40 | THE DEEP LEARNING REVOLUTION]**

The real explosion happened when three things came together:

More data. More powerful hardware. And better algorithms.

Especially GPUs.

Originally designed mainly for graphics, GPUs turned out to be extremely useful for performing the huge number of mathematical calculations needed to train neural networks.

Then, in 2012, a neural network called AlexNet achieved a major breakthrough in image recognition.

It dramatically outperformed traditional approaches in a major computer-vision competition.

This showed researchers that deep neural networks could scale much further than many people expected.

And AI research accelerated rapidly.

---

**[3:40 - 4:25 | TRANSFORMERS AND GENERATIVE AI]**

Then came another huge breakthrough.

2017.

Researchers at Google published a paper called:

"Attention Is All You Need."

It introduced the Transformer architecture.

Transformers changed how AI could process sequences of information, especially language.

Instead of simply reading one word at a time, the model could learn relationships between different parts of a sentence.

And this architecture became the foundation for many modern AI systems.

Large language models were trained on enormous amounts of text.

They learned statistical patterns in language.

They learned how words, concepts, and structures relate to each other.

And eventually, something surprising happened.

These models weren't just completing sentences.

They could write essays, generate code, translate languages, summarize information, answer questions, and interact conversationally.

---

**[4:25 - 4:50 | CHATGPT AND THE AI BOOM]**

Then, in November 2022, ChatGPT was released publicly.

And suddenly, AI wasn't something only researchers or large companies were experimenting with.

Millions of ordinary people could simply open a website, type a question, and have a conversation with an AI.

This triggered an enormous wave of interest in generative AI.

Companies began building AI systems for coding, images, audio, video, research, education, business, and much more.

AI had officially entered everyday life.

---

**[4:50 - 5:15 | HOW AI BECAME INTELLIGENT]**

But here's the most important part.

AI didn't suddenly become intelligent because someone programmed intelligence into it.

Modern AI became powerful because researchers discovered ways to let machines learn patterns from enormous amounts of data, using increasingly powerful mathematical models and hardware.

The basic loop is surprisingly simple:

Data, Training, Prediction, Error, Adjustment, Repeat.

Do that billions of times with huge models, massive datasets, and enormous computing power, and surprisingly capable behavior can emerge.

---

**[5:15 - 5:30 | ENDING]**

And that's how we went from machines that could only follow instructions,

to machines that can generate language, images, code, music, and increasingly complex reasoning.

But we're still at the beginning.

The next question isn't just:

"Can machines become intelligent?"

It's:

"How intelligent can they become?"

And that, is a story we're still writing.
